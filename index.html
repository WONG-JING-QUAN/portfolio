01 INTERNSHIP
Data Engineering · Practicum Project

SGX Insider Data Pipeline & Stock Screener Rhee Analytics Sdn. Bhd.

An automated pipeline that collects, extracts and validates SGX insider filings (Forms 1, 3, 5 and 6), checked by an audit of 49,632 PDF filings, and feeds an Excel stock screener rebuilt as 85 named formulas.

PythonSeleniumpdfplumberPower QueryLAMBDAVBA
Case study: summary, role, outcome & reflection
Format
Infographics, screenshots, a practicum proposal and 13 weekly reports
Summary
Directors, chief executive officers and substantial shareholders of SGX-listed companies must file disclosure forms when their shareholdings change, and these filings are published as PDF announcements. The information is useful in equity research, but it is spread across thousands of individual PDF documents and cannot be analysed directly. At Rhee Analytics Sdn. Bhd. I developed an automated end-to-end data pipeline that collects, extracts, validates and processes SGX insider disclosure data (Forms 1, 3, 5 and 6, including XFA dynamic PDFs), together with an Excel-based stock screener that presents the processed data for fast filtering and analysis. The project reduces repetitive manual work, improves the accuracy and consistency of the data, and gives analysts a practical decision-support tool based on insider activity, share buy-backs and market data.
My role
Data Analyst Intern and main developer of the SGX insider disclosure pipeline and the SGX Stock Screener, working under my industrial supervisor
Skills / tools
Python (pandas, pypdf, pdfplumber, Selenium, Requests), DuckDB SQL, Power Query, Excel LAMBDA formulas, VBA, Windows batch files and Task Scheduler; data validation, regression testing and technical writing
Outcome / impact
The pipeline runs unattended every weeknight. An audit of 49,632 PDF filings identified 39,516 unique submissions, and a review tool links every doubtful record to its original PDF. The screener was rebuilt from one formula of about 4,000 characters into 85 named formulas, with new filters, sorting and a live match count.
Reflection
Most of the serious problems I met did not cause errors; they quietly produced wrong numbers. One checkbox option, for example, had never been captured in the whole history because its tick was lost during the PDF-to-text conversion. The main lesson I take away is that good decisions depend on reliable data. Checking results against the source, testing every change and writing clear documentation are habits I will keep in my future work.

02 INTERNSHIP
Performance · Excel & Python

HKEX Stock Screener, Version 3 Rhee Analytics Sdn. Bhd.

A rebuild of a screener covering about 2,780 Hong Kong companies. Opening time fell from about five minutes to a few seconds, and the file shrank to about a fifth of its size.

Excel VBAPower QueryPythonDuckDB
Case study: summary, role, outcome & reflection
Format
Screenshots and the Week 8 report
Summary
The HKEX Stock Screener is an Excel tool that covers about 2,780 Hong Kong companies. When I took it on, the workbook took around five minutes to open and sometimes crashed when closing. I tested the possible causes one at a time and found that the delay came from the huge number of formula cells stored in the file, not from the calculations themselves. Version 3 stores the formulas as plain values when the file is saved and restores them when it opens, and moves the heavy data preparation out of Excel into Python and DuckDB, with a REFRESH DATA button that prepares one clean data file in under half a minute. I also added switches for unique stocks and price returns, share type and share count columns, clearer buttons and a settings sheet for non-technical users.
My role
Rebuilt the screener from my industrial supervisor’s task brief, then tested it and documented it in a README with a version history
Skills / tools
Excel VBA, Power Query, Python, DuckDB; performance testing and scenario-based testing
Outcome / impact
The workbook now opens in a few seconds instead of about five minutes, the file is about a fifth of its former size, and a data refresh takes under half a minute.
Reflection
Measuring first turned a vague “slow file” complaint into one clear cause. When Excel showed a “busy” error during recalculation, I added a helper that waits and retries, and I walked through ten realistic user scenarios to find hidden bugs before users did. I learned that moving heavy work to the right tool is often better than making formulas faster, and that a tool is only easy to maintain if non-technical users can understand its settings.

03 FYP
Optimisation · Final Year Project

Optimizing Dietary Plans for Athletic Weight Gain in Malaysia A Linear Programming Approach

A Simplex LP model optimised 10 Malaysian foods for hypertrophy. For the baseline athlete, it achieved a 2,760 kcal total intake for RM 15.01/day. Tested across a 10-athlete cohort, it mathematically proved hitting Vitamin D targets exclusively via whole foods violates national serving limits.

Linear ProgrammingSimplexSports NutritionDecision Science
Case study: summary, role, outcome & reflection
Format
Academic project report (SQQZK4993, A252) with an Excel Simplex LP model and a project poster (PDF)
Summary
Managing nutrition is important for athletic weight gain, but manual diet planning is inefficient and not economical in the Malaysian context. This study formulated a Linear Programming model, solved with the Simplex algorithm, to minimise the daily cost of a high-calorie diet for athletic hypertrophy using foods available in Malaysia, while meeting sports nutrition standards and the maximum daily serving sizes in the Malaysian Dietary Guidelines 2020. For the baseline athlete the model produced a diet of exactly 2,760 kcal at RM 15.01 a day, and scenario analyses re-solved the diet for a 10-athlete cohort with allergies and food aversions. The optimisation also uncovered a Vitamin D infeasibility, showing that whole foods alone cannot reach the target within serving limits, so supplementation is needed.
My role
Sole author, supervised by Dr Sahubar Ali bin Mohamed Nadhar Khan
Skills / tools & data
Linear programming and scenario analysis in Microsoft Excel Simplex LP; nutrition data from USDA FoodData Central and Open Food Facts; serving limits from the Malaysian Dietary Guidelines 2020; food prices from Shopee Malaysia
Outcome / impact
An exact 2,760 kcal diet for the baseline athlete at RM 15.01 a day, re-solved for a 10-athlete cohort with allergies and taste aversions. The model also exposed a Vitamin D infeasibility: whole foods alone cannot meet the target within serving limits, so supplementation is needed.
Reflection
Compiling and standardising nutrition data from several sources, including converting US volume units to grams, took the most effort. Prices were recorded once, so the cost is static; the report recommends pulling live grocery prices through web scraping or APIs and turning the model into a web dashboard athletes can use. That kind of automated data pipeline is exactly what I went on to build during my internship.
